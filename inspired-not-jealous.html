How to get inspired instead of jealous

Somewhere in a room full of people who all seem sharper, faster, more sure of themselves than I feel, there's a specific sensation I've come to know well. Small-fish-in-a-big-pond. Not quite jealousy, not quite insecurity — something closer to shrinking.

I used to treat that feeling as information about my own limits. If everyone around me seemed more capable, that must mean something true about where I stood. I've since decided that's a bad use of the feeling. It's real, but it's not evidence.

What's helped, slowly, is treating the feeling as a signal to look closer rather than look away. If someone's ability makes me feel small, it's usually because some part of me actually wants what they have — and that want, if I let it, points somewhere useful. Jealousy without curiosity just corrodes. Jealousy with curiosity turns into a question: what exactly are they doing that I'm not?

Jealousy is just inspiration that hasn't asked itself any questions yet.

That question is more actionable than the feeling ever was. Sometimes the answer is a skill I haven't built. Sometimes it's a risk I haven't taken. Sometimes, honestly, the answer is that they've just had more time or more luck, and there's nothing to extract from that except patience.

I don't think I'll ever fully stop feeling small in rooms like that. But I've stopped assuming it means I don't belong in them. Feeling behind someone is not the same as being behind them — most of the time it just means I'm standing close enough to see clearly.
