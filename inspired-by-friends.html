The power of being inspired by your friends

There's a particular kind of energy that comes from watching someone you actually know do something remarkable. Not a stranger on a screen, not a name in a headline — someone whose voice you'd recognise, who you've sat across a table from. When they do something extraordinary, it stops feeling abstract. It starts feeling possible.

I've been lucky to have friends like that. People who debated better than me, wrote better than me, walked into rooms with more certainty than I felt I had any right to. For a while I treated that as a kind of ranking system — where I stood relative to them. That framing didn't serve me well, and it didn't serve the friendships either.

What changed things was noticing what actually happened in the moments right after someone I loved did something impressive. It wasn't envy that showed up first. It was momentum. A kind of "if they can, maybe I can too" that had nothing to do with competing and everything to do with permission.

Inspiration is what admiration looks like once you stop needing it to be about you.

I think the trick — if there is one — is letting someone else's ambition raise your ceiling without turning it into your yardstick. Their timeline was never going to be mine anyway. What I can borrow is the proof that it's possible, not the schedule on which they did it.

The friends who've pushed me forward the most were rarely trying to. They were just doing their own thing, fully, in front of me. That was enough.
