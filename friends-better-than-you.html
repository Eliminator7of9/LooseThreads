Why having friends better than you isn't always good

There's advice that gets repeated so often it stops sounding like advice and starts sounding like fact: surround yourself with people better than you, and you'll rise to meet them. I believed that for a long time. I'm less sure of it now.

It's true that ambitious friends raise the ceiling of what feels possible. Watching someone you love do something extraordinary — win the debate, land the offer, publish the piece — makes the extraordinary feel reachable in a way it didn't before. That part of the advice holds.

What it leaves out is the other side of proximity: the quiet, constant measuring. Not jealousy exactly — closer to a background hum of comparison that never fully switches off, even when you're genuinely, uncomplicatedly happy for them.

The people who make you feel most capable and the people who make you feel most behind are, often, the same people.

I don't think the answer is fewer impressive friends. I think it's being honest that admiration and insecurity can share the same room, and that neither one cancels the other out. You can be proud of someone and still need a minute alone with your own timeline afterward.
